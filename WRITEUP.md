# FINN: covering note (draft)

**What I built.** FINN treats an investor's day as triage, not a feed. *Today* opens with one sentence ("3 things need you"), then gives full cards for material filings, one-liners for things worth a glance, and folds the routine paperwork away by category. You clear cards as you deal with them, until you reach inbox zero. Each card arrives pre-digested: results show Revenue/EBITDA/PBT/PAT QoQ and YoY, a management change shows who the new person is, and any filing the market reacted to shows the move and the volume multiple. Alerts fire on thresholds the user sets for price and volume, and on material filings in the categories they watch. They can reach a phone through ntfy.

On the pull side I went deep on one thing: **promised vs delivered**. FINN reads the last five concall transcripts, extracts each concrete commitment management made, verifies every quote verbatim against the transcript, and scores each promise as kept / partly / missed / pending against the reported numbers. The output is a credibility verdict and the one thing to check on the next call. The other lenses (trajectory, balance sheet, cash conversion) are computed straight from the reported tables. Business snapshot, narrative vs numbers and bull/bear are grounded only in the annual report's MD&A and the latest call. It works on any NSE ticker that screener covers.

**Key decisions.**
- *The model goes only where judgement is needed.* Classification, ranking and every financial ratio are deterministic code. Routine filings never reach an LLM. Flash-Lite does bulk extraction, and Flash is used only for the two reasoning steps (judging promises, writing the thesis).
- *Models copy, code computes, and code checks the copy.* The results card has the model copy raw line items; EBITDA, growth and margins are computed in Python, each quarter is reconciled against the statement's own arithmetic, and revenue is cross-checked against screener.
- *Distrust the model's quotes.* Guidance items whose quote can't be found in the source are dropped, and the UI says how many were dropped.
- *Cache everything.* Every call is keyed by content hash, so a second refresh costs ₹0. Spend is visible in the app.
- *Ingestion is separate from serving.* NSE blocks cloud IPs, so ingestion runs locally and the deployed app serves the snapshot. Research on new tickers still runs live on the server.

**What running it on real data changed.** The first version was written without network access, so the first live week was a test. Most of what it found was the kind of error that looks fine on a screen:
- *Titan's EBITDA came out at +95% YoY.* Every number the model copied was right. The formula was wrong: a ₹671 cr fair-value gain sat inside total income. Non-operating income is now derived from the printed total-income line and kept out of EBITDA (true figure +59%), and the card flags it.
- *SBI's scanned results shifted columns.* The model put the exceptional item in the wrong quarters, both from the text and from the PDF image. Every results card is now checked against the statement's own arithmetic, quarter by quarter. A failure triggers one PDF re-read, a column shift is repaired only when the printed numbers prove it, and anything still inconsistent is flagged rather than shown as fact.
- *The digest put the wrong things on top.* Seven items were in "Needs you", including a vice-chairman's routine retirement that had borrowed the results-day price move. After crediting a reaction only to the filing that caused it, treating a deal's completion as a follow-up to its agreement, and letting the model's "paperwork" vote count, five items remain, and each one is a real reason to act.
- *Smaller things:* the MD&A reader was reading a CEO letter; intraday prices were stored as the close; the 2.5 Gemini models were closed to new keys partway through the build, and 3.5-flash's free daily quota ran out after a day of research runs, so the smart model is now 3.8-flash; an overloaded or exhausted model now falls back to the other tier instead of crashing the run, and malformed JSON is retried rather than cached.

**Honest gaps.** Web search grounding for management bios returns 429 on the free API tier, so the incoming person's background comes only from the profile the filing itself carries; when a filing has none (Asian Paints' postal-ballot result for its incoming chairman), the card shows none. Trent's concall entries have no transcripts on screener, so its promised-vs-delivered view is empty. Results cards were checked by hand for Titan, Bharti, SBI and a lakh-denominated small cap, not across the market. Prices are end-of-day, so alerts lag the move by a session.

**Left out deliberately.** BSE announcements (NSE covers the portfolio), real web push (ntfy gives real phone notifications in ten lines), LinkedIn scraping (the filing's mandated profile covers the must-have, and grounded search adds to it on tiers that allow it), a vector DB, and user accounts.

**Next.** Guidance tracking across years, with a running credibility score per management team; an evening "what changed in your thesis" brief that diffs new filings against the last research view; results-day alerts at release time rather than end of day; and a portfolio-level view of where the book is most exposed to a missed promise.
